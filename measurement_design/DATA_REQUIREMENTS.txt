GRUNDDATEN, SIGNALQUALITAET UND ZAEHLSTATISTIK
Read-only audit of the three 3% relative + 3% peak-referenced absolute datasets.
Signal thresholds use the synthetic true generating sigma, only for diagnosis.
They are not solver parameter selection, rank tests or a minimum sample count.
The solver uses the original plug-in sigma based on observed signal.
M1: 624 measurements, peak 14181.1 #/cm3, absolute sigma 425.432 #/cm3; signal >=1/3/5 sigma at 336/278/246 points; 141 negative raw readings clipped.
M2: 624 measurements, peak 14190.9 #/cm3, absolute sigma 425.727 #/cm3; signal >=1/3/5 sigma at 364/305/274 points; 114 negative raw readings clipped.
M3: 624 measurements, peak 14348.6 #/cm3, absolute sigma 430.458 #/cm3; signal >=1/3/5 sigma at 396/346/305 points; 122 negative raw readings clipped.

More sampled points with signals below the absolute noise floor need not improve inversion.
Clipping negative Gaussian readings makes blank-channel errors biased and non-Gaussian.
This limitation is retained for comparability; actual likelihood/covariance needs calibration.
The current absolute sigma is independent across points. A common offset or calibration bias
has correlated errors and cannot be reduced as 1/sqrt(number of readings).

Required information for one inversion:
- Concentrations/raw counts with U, rotation, timestamps and dwell/scan history.
- Flow rates, geometry, temperature/pressure/viscosity, charge distribution and detector response.
- The density/reference convention defining dst; dst is not automatically a volume diameter.
- Blank/zero and repeat measurements to estimate additive noise, signal-dependent variance,
  covariance/drift, counting efficiency and instrument/background offsets.
- A calibrated stationary or dynamic transfer function, depending on acquisition.
- Independent total/marginal measurements only if genuinely acquired with their uncertainty.
  Known simulated truth must not become a measurement constraint.

No universal number of calibration repetitions can be inferred without a precision target.
For independent Gaussian repeated readings at one setting, the approximate relative standard
error of a sample standard deviation is 1/sqrt(2*(r-1)): r=14/51/201 repetitions correspond
to about 20%/10%/5% precision in sigma (one standard error, not a confidence guarantee).
A 95% interval would require more repetitions; drift and correlation reduce effective count.
Use several blank and representative low/mid/high-signal settings, rather than pool different
concentrations as if they were repetitions of one mean.

Ideal Poisson counting bound, not the present Gaussian benchmark:
relative counting sigma=1/sqrt(expected counts), expected counts=Q*c*t.
5%:400; 3%:1112; 1%:10000 expected counts per point. With Q=0.3 L/min=5 cm3/s
and classified c=10 #/cm3, the ideal times are 8/22.24/200 s per point.
At fixed total dwell time, increasing rows divides the available counts among more settings;
it is therefore a different comparison from fixed per-row sigma in the design study.
Concentration is AFTER classification. Upstream N=10^5 #/cm3 is not a detector count rate.
Settling, tubing, efficiency, dead time and correlated calibration error are absent from this bound.

User-specified CPC limit: maximum 50 Hz, hence minimum timebin 20 ms.
The transfer function will be adapted for a dynamic scan. This study does not impose a
stationary-kernel minimum scan duration on that future dynamic acquisition.
At 5 cm3/s sensor flow a 20-ms bin has expected counts=0.1*c (ideal detection).
A single bin reaches ideal 3% Poisson sigma only at c>=11111.12 #/cm3 after classification;
5% needs c>=4000 #/cm3. At the present 14181..14349 #/cm3 peak, there are
about 1418..1435 expected counts/bin and ideal Poisson sigma about 2.64..2.66%.
Low-signal bins need longer integrated count time or aggregation. During a varying scan,
aggregation must also integrate the corresponding dynamic kernel instead of assigning
all counts to the centre voltage. 50-Hz storage is not a statement about independent samples.
